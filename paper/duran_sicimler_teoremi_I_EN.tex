\begin{abstract}
This study reinterprets the relationship between the vibration of strings and time, entropy and perception.
Classical string theory maintains that the fundamental building blocks of the universe are vibrating strings.
This theorem, however, proposes that humans can only perceive vibrating strings, and cannot discern the
``perfect order'' that emerges when vibration ceases.
From this point of view, black holes are passages that halt the vibration of strings and return the universe to perfection.
Hence black holes are not annihilation, but the physical reflection of a transition into a different dimension.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Modern physics assumes a cosmic arrow of time beginning with the Big Bang (Big Bang).
That moment is the point at which the first vibrations of strings began, that is, energy passed from an orderly state to a disorderly one.
In this process, the increase of entropy is the cosmic-scale expression of deviation from perfection.

\section{The Stationary String Postulate}
This theorem proposes that when the vibration of strings halts, time and entropy lose their meaning.
When vibration is nullified ($\omega = 0$), strings form a perfect order.
In that case energy exchange, wave propagation and even photon motion disappear.
Since the human capacity of perception can only observe vibrating systems (energy), this perfect order appears ``dark''.

\section{Black Holes and Dimensional Transition}
Black holes are commonly defined as regions where gravity is so intense that not even light can escape.
This theorem, however, proposes that black holes are at their core structures that halt the vibration of strings.
In that case black holes are passages where energy and time end and perfection is restored.
Every black hole in the universe functions as a ``door'' opening onto another dimension of perfect order.

\section{Cosmological Consequences}
This approach maintains that a pure and perfect order existed at the beginning of the universe,
that time and entropy emerged with the vibration of strings,
and that black holes reverse this process.
The ``end'' of the universe is therefore not a collapse,
but the recovery of the perfect state of the beginning.

\section{Conclusion}
The Stationary Strings Theorem proposes that beyond the perceivable limits of the physical universe,
a perfect order where vibration has ceased may exist.
This order depends neither on energy nor on time.
Since human perception cannot reach this dimension, black holes appear dark to us;
but at their core, they are the regions where the universe is reborn.
